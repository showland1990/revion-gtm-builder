# Business case and ROI

> The business case is not a vendor spreadsheet claiming 900% ROI. It is the customer's own argument for why change is worth funding now.

## Inputs must come from discovery

Use:
- current process
- frequency/volume
- people affected
- time/cost
- risk
- delay
- strategic priority
- proposed outcome
- customer-provided assumptions

Label estimates.

## Value categories

### Efficiency
hours/cost removed

### Capacity
more work without proportional headcount

### Speed
release/migration/project acceleration

### Revenue
enabled/accelerated revenue, only when causal link is credible

### Risk
incident, compliance, reliability or security exposure

### Strategic
competitive position, platform standardization, transformation priority

## Cost of delay

Often stronger than abstract ROI:
- what does each month of inaction cost?
- what deadline is missed?
- what risk remains?
- what project remains blocked?

## Conservative model

Show:
- baseline
- assumption
- expected improvement
- value range
- product cost
- implementation cost
- payback
- sensitivities

Avoid false precision.

## Internal narrative

Champion should be able to say:
1. this problem is real
2. it costs/blocks X
3. we need change by Y
4. we evaluated alternatives
5. this approach met our criteria
6. investment is Z
7. expected outcome/payback is credible
8. risks are managed

## Output

Write business-case section in opportunity file and optionally a reusable account-specific one-pager.
