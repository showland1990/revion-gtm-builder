# Engagement and retention

> Retention is evidence that the problem and value recur. If users do not come back, growth eventually becomes replacement.

## Choose the right retention cadence

Ask how often the job naturally occurs: daily, weekly, monthly, event-driven or project-based.
Do not force DAU/MAU on a monthly or event-driven product.

## Define retained behavior

Retention should mean: "the customer repeatedly receives the core value".
Not login, page view or email open.

Define at user and account levels when both matter.

## Retention analysis

Break down by:
- signup cohort
- activation achieved/not achieved
- use case
- ICP segment
- company size
- acquisition channel
- plan
- integration/setup path

Look for early drop, cliff after initial project, stable retained core, resurrection, and multi-user vs single-user differences.

## Diagnose causes

Potential causes:
- wrong ICP
- weak activation
- one-time job
- poor reliability
- missing integration
- insufficient recurring trigger
- no collaboration loop
- value not strong enough
- product replaced by workflow/status quo
- champion left

## Engagement loops

Build only loops that deepen value:
- recurring workflow
- automation
- notifications tied to genuine events
- collaboration
- saved state/history
- integration embedding
- generated assets others consume
- team standards/governance

Avoid notification spam as a substitute for product value.

## Output

Update `plg-model.md` with natural cadence, retention definition, cohort baseline, retention drivers, churn causes, engagement loops and experiments.
