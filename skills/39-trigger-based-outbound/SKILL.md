# Trigger-based outbound

> Good outbound answers four questions: why this account, why now, why this person, why us.

Do not start with email copy.

## Outbound thesis

For each target segment define:
- trigger
- likely problem
- likely owner
- likely business consequence
- credible proof
- useful CTA

## Trigger hierarchy

Strong:
- explicit product intent
- known usage
- active evaluation
- hiring/project announcement
- incident/regulatory deadline
- contract/migration event

Medium:
- role change
- growth signal
- stack change
- strategic initiative

Weak:
- generic firmographic fit with no timing evidence

Use weak signals only for hypothesis testing, not fake personalization.

## Persona hypothesis

Before outreach specify:
- role in buying committee
- problem hypothesis
- why they would care personally
- what proof matters
- expected objection
- appropriate CTA

## Message standard

An outbound message should contain:
1. relevant observation or reason for reaching out
2. problem hypothesis
3. credible outcome/proof
4. low-friction next step

Avoid:
- fake familiarity
- generic compliments
- over-claiming
- long product explanations
- deceptive subject lines
- pretending a guessed problem is known fact

## Sequence design

Vary purpose, not just wording:
- hypothesis
- proof
- insight
- alternative angle
- close-the-loop

Define channel mix only where appropriate:
- email
- LinkedIn/social
- phone
- founder/executive
- product/community touch
- event/partner

## Measure downstream

Track:
- delivered
- positive/relevant response
- qualified conversation
- opportunity
- pipeline
- win
- ACV

Do not optimize replies divorced from qualification.
