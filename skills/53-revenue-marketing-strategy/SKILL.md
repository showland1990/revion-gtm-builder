# Revenue marketing strategy

> Revenue marketing is not a channel calendar. It is the system that creates demand, captures existing intent, advances buying journeys, and compounds customer proof into more revenue.

Read first:
1. `docs/gtm-cofounder/gtm-context.md`
2. `docs/gtm-cofounder/gtm-roadmap.md`
3. `docs/gtm-cofounder/plg-model.md` if PLG exists
4. `docs/gtm-cofounder/sales-model.md` if sales-led exists
5. `docs/gtm-cofounder/messaging.md` or messaging-house output
6. existing marketing/channel analytics if available

## Start with revenue architecture, not channels

Define:
- business objective
- primary ICP/segments
- buying committee
- PLG vs sales-led relationship
- current revenue constraint
- existing demand vs demand creation need
- self-serve vs human conversion path
- customer expansion motion

## Four jobs of revenue marketing

### 1. Create demand
Make the target market understand:
- the problem
- why old approaches fail
- why now
- what better looks like

### 2. Capture demand
Be discoverable when buyers/users actively seek:
- category
- problem
- alternatives
- integrations
- pricing
- proof

### 3. Progress demand
Help accounts/users move from awareness to:
- activation
- evaluation
- opportunity
- purchase

### 4. Compound demand
Turn:
- customers
- proof
- advocates
- community
- ecosystem
into future acquisition and expansion.

## Demand states

Use states rather than a generic lead funnel:

```text
Unaware / latent pain
→ Problem aware
→ Solution / category aware
→ Product aware
→ Evaluating
→ Activated / engaged
→ Buying
→ Customer
→ Expansion-ready
→ Advocate
```

Map each state to:
- question
- message
- proof
- channel
- CTA
- owner
- measurable progression

## Source architecture

Revenue marketing may feed:
- self-serve signup
- PQL/PQA
- inbound sales
- target account engagement
- expansion
- advocacy

All should connect to one commercial model.
